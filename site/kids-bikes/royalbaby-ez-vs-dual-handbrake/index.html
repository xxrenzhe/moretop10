RoyalBaby EZ vs Dual Handbrake Kids Bike

PN
Written by Priya Nair Last Updated: 2026-10-23
Kids Bikes

A practical head-to-head of two kids bikes on our live shortlist. We compare real trade-offs—capacity, cleanup, and day-two habits—not brochure superlatives.

Use this page to decide which job each model wins, then open the live kids bikes comparison for scores and Check price links that reflect current retailer offers.

Neither pick is universally “better.” The right choice depends on household size, how often you cook or clean, and which features you will actually use weekly.

Choose the RoyalBaby EZ Kids Bike (12–20 inch Balance & Pedal, $239.99) if your child still needs a balance phase before pedals and you want one tool-free convert. Pick the Dual Handbrake beginners bike (12–20 inch, $149.99) if they are ready to pedal with two handbrakes and you want the lower Best value snapshot. Skip the Dual Handbrake model if they cannot coast yet; skip the EZ if convert hardware is money you will never use.

Key insights

  • Learning path: EZ starts as a balance bike and switches to pedals with a one-button / tool-less crank design. Dual Handbrake is a pedal bike from day one with kids-specified two-hand brakes.
  • Price bracket: EZ sits at $239.99 (Top pick); Dual Handbrake sits at $149.99 (Best value)—same 12–20 inch title range class, different jobs.
  • Brakes: Dual Handbrake emphasizes short-pull levers and no coaster-brake fight while learning pedal position. EZ focuses on convert geometry; confirm brake details on each retailer page.
  • Assembly: Both list high pre-assembly (EZ ~98%, Dual Handbrake ~95%) with tools or tool-less convert noted on the listing.
  • Review depth: EZ shows a thicker review count on the shortlist snapshot (~3000 at 4.6) than Dual Handbrake (27 at 4.2)—useful signal, not a lab score.

How does the learning path differ?

The RoyalBaby EZ (ASIN B0BCQ1VJDP) is built around “two types of bike”: balance first for confidence, then a seamless transform into a pedal bike. Listing copy calls out reduced frame weight vs average, child-friendly geometry, low standover, and adjustable saddle/handlebar so feet can touch the ground early.

The Dual Handbrake beginners bike (ASIN B0CPDZ7R5P) is a Freestyle-class pedal bike with dual handbrakes. Listing copy argues that coaster brakes get in the way when kids pedal backward to find pedal position, so two-hand brakes with short-pull levers keep stopping power without that fight.

How do price and roles compare?

  • RoyalBaby EZ — Top pick: $239.99 snapshot, rating 4.6, ~3000 reviews. Fun accessories called out (jet-engine bottle, bell, stem cover). CPSC and EN-71 language on the listing.
  • Dual Handbrake — Best value: $149.99 snapshot, rating 4.2, 27 reviews. Sporty metallic paint, sealed bearing, jet-engine bottle, ~15-minute assembly claim, lifetime frame warranty language on the listing.

If budget is the hard gate and pedaling is already the goal, Best value wins. If one bike must cover balance and pedals, Top pick EZ wins.

Who should pick which

### Pick the RoyalBaby EZ if:

  • Your child still scoots / glides and is not ready for pedals every ride.
  • You want tool-less convert instead of buying a second bike later.
  • You prefer the thicker review history on the shortlist snapshot.

### Pick the Dual Handbrake bike if:

  • They already want to pedal and practice two-hand braking.
  • You want the lowest shortlist snapshot in the 12–20 beginners lane.
  • Convert hardware would sit unused in the garage.

FAQ

Can the Dual Handbrake bike work as a balance bike?

No—shortlist copy positions it as a pedal bike with dual handbrakes. For balance-first learning, use EZ / EZ-Pro / EZ-One converts on the [Kids Bikes comparison](/kids-bikes/comparison/).

Is EZ always better because it costs more?

No. Pay for convert only if you will use the balance phase. Confident pedalers often do better on Dual Handbrake or Freestyle cards at lower snapshots.
