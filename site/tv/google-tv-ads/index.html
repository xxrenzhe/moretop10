As of 2026-10-22, Android TV and Google TV are the platforms where a software path can reduce launcher ads — boot splashes, home-row recommendations, and some promoted shelves. Start on how to remove smart TV ads, confirm the build on supported models, and use launchers plus ADB troubleshooting before you shop. Come to a replacement only after install fails or debugging will not stay on — then when software will not install. A Fire Stick is not that path; we will not recommend one.

Software first

The honest order is install, confirm the model, then troubleshoot. The unofficial CleanYourTV project is third-party open-source and non-official — MoreTop10 is not affiliated and does not host the software. Compare it with stock Google TV, Projectivy, and vendor overlays on the launcher guide. A replacement home app can hide boot and home-row promos. It cannot remove ads inside Netflix or YouTube. It cannot add ADB to Samsung Tizen or LG webOS.

  1. Enable Developer options and USB debugging. Keep the TV and computer on the same subnet.
  2. Follow the unofficial project’s current README. We do not mirror those commands here.
  3. Confirm the retail name against the models table. Absence is “unknown,” not “impossible.”
  4. If ADB cannot see the set, debugging resets, or the launcher will not stay default, use ADB not working before you open a checkout tab.

What a launcher can change

In scope: boot / splash promos, home-row recommendations, some “because you watched” shelves. Out of scope: streaming-app ads, paid tiers, and HDMI devices you plug in. If the frustration is “this OS phones home,” that is a privacy or non-smart decision, not a new APK.

When to leave for a whole-TV replacement

  • The set is actually Samsung Tizen or LG webOS — leave for Samsung or LG, then the alternatives decision tree.
  • Developer options are locked or wiped on every reboot after a clean retry.
  • You already followed the unofficial notes and the launcher will not stay default.
  • You are shopping anyway and want Google TV called out versus Fire. Fire panels are not an “ad-free” upgrade — see Fire TV ads.
